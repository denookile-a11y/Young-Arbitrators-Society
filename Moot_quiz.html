STATEMENT OF FACTS

  1. Claimant, Savanna Carbon Solutions Ltd ("Savanna"), is a company incorporated under the laws of Kenya and engaged in the development, verification, and sale of voluntary carbon credits generated through nature-based projects in Kenya. Its flagship project is the Mau Forest Carbon Conservation Project ("Mau Project"), covering approximately 37,000 hectares of the Mau Forest Complex, Rift Valley. The Mau Project operates under the REDD+ framework and has been certified by Verra under the Verified Carbon Standard ("VCS") since 2019, generating Verified Carbon Units ("VCUs"). Each VCU represents one metric tonne of CO₂ equivalent reduced or avoided through verified forest conservation activities.

  2. Respondent, DigiCarbon International PLC ("DigiCarbon"), is a company incorporated under the laws of Novaterra. It operates a digital platform called "CarbonChain" for the purchase, tokenization, and resale of voluntary carbon credits to institutional buyers. Central to the CarbonChain platform is DigiCarbon's proprietary artificial intelligence system, "CarbonAI", which it uses to verify the quality and compliance of carbon credits submitted by sellers. DigiCarbon also issues a proprietary blockchain-based virtual asset, the "GreenToken" (symbol: GRT), which it uses as its primary payment instrument for carbon credit purchases. GRT is traded on digital exchanges including CarbonChain.

  3. VCS-certified VCUs are listed on the Verra Registry, an internationally recognised and publicly accessible database. Verra, the administrator of the VCS, audits and registers VCUs following rigorous independent third-party verification by accredited Validation and Verification Bodies ("VVBs"). Under Kenya's Climate Change Act, 2016 (as amended by the Climate Change Amendment Act, 2023) and the Carbon Markets Regulations, 2024 ("CMR 2024"), VCS-certified VCUs are recognised as valid voluntary carbon credits.

  4. GRT is not designated as legal tender in Kenya or Novaterra. As at 15 March 2023 (date of contracting), 1 GRT was equivalent to approximately USD 2.00. By 15 May 2024 (the date Respondent purported to terminate the Smart Carbon Purchase Agreement), 1 GRT traded at approximately USD 2.50.

  5. DigiCarbon approached Savanna in January 2023 expressing interest in procuring large quantities of Mau Project VCUs for resale on the CarbonChain platform. DigiCarbon explained that it required high-quality, VCS-certified VCUs and that its CarbonAI verification system applied "Verra's VCS methodology as the definitive evaluation standard." During negotiations, Savanna inquired about the specific parameters of CarbonAI's verification algorithm to ensure compatibility with its VCUs. DigiCarbon's Chief Technology Officer, Ms. Yuki Tanaka, declined to disclose the proprietary algorithm but confirmed in writing on 20 February 2023 that CarbonAI "uses the Verra VCS methodology as its evaluation framework" and that "a valid Verra Registry certificate and third-party verification report are the primary inputs to CarbonAI's assessment." (Claimant Exhibit C 1)

  6. On 15 March 2023, the parties entered into the Smart Carbon Purchase Agreement ("SCPA") (Claimant Exhibit C 2), under which Savanna agreed to sell 500,000 VCUs from the Mau Project to DigiCarbon in two tranches of 250,000 VCUs each. The price was 12 GRT per VCU, totalling 6,000,000 GRT. Payment was to be effected automatically by an embedded smart contract on the CarbonChain platform: upon CarbonAI's verification of each tranche, the smart contract would auto-release GRT payment to Savanna's designated digital wallet. Tranche 1 was due by 1 October 2023; Tranche 2 by 1 April 2024. The SCPA is governed by the laws of Kenya.

  7. On 1 October 2023, Savanna submitted the first tranche of 250,000 VCUs through the CarbonChain platform, together with corresponding Verra Registry transfer documentation and independent verification reports. (Claimant Exhibit C 3)

  8. On 5 October 2023, CarbonAI generated an automated Rejection Report ("Rejection Report") flagging 180,000 of the 250,000 submitted VCUs as non-compliant under three proprietary parameters: a "Non-Additionality Risk Index" of 0.72 (against a threshold of 0.60), a "Permanence Concern Index" of 0.65 (against a threshold of 0.55), and an "Anomalous Baseline Deviation" of 0.58 (against a threshold of 0.50). The smart contract auto-released 840,000 GRT in payment for the 70,000 approved VCUs only. No payment was released for the 180,000 rejected VCUs. (Claimant Exhibit C 4)

  9. The three parameters cited in the Rejection Report are not standard Verra VCS reporting metrics and their thresholds do not correspond to any published VCS Methodology. Savanna engaged Bureau Veritas Kenya Ltd, a Verra-accredited VVB, to independently re-verify all 250,000 Tranche 1 VCUs. Bureau Veritas issued its report on 15 November 2023 confirming that all 250,000 VCUs were fully compliant with VCS Methodology VM0015 as applied to the Mau Project and that there were no additionality, permanence, or baseline concerns. (Claimant Exhibit C 5)

  10. Between November and December 2023, the parties exchanged correspondence about the CarbonAI rejection. Savanna requested access to CarbonAI's evaluation parameters to understand the discrepancy with Verra's certification. DigiCarbon refused, citing trade secret protection. DigiCarbon maintained that Clause 8.2 of the SCPA made CarbonAI's determination conclusive for payment purposes. Savanna submitted a formal request to Verra on 10 December 2023 to review the CarbonAI rejection in light of the Bureau Veritas report, as provided for in Clause 8.3 of the SCPA. On 20 December 2023, Verra responded that it does not adjudicate disputes between sellers and buyers regarding third-party AI verification systems and declined to intervene. No resolution was reached between the parties. (Claimant Exhibit C 6)

  11. On 1 January 2024, the NCIA Arbitration Rules 2024 ("NCIA Rules 2024") came into force. Rule 1.4 thereof provides: "These Rules shall apply to any arbitration administered by the NCIA that is commenced on or after 1 January 2024, unless the parties have otherwise agreed in writing." The 2024 Rules introduced, among other provisions, Rule 38 (AI Systems in Arbitral Proceedings), Rule 39 (Smart Contracts as Evidence), and Rule 40 (Digital Asset Valuation in Damages).

  12. On 1 April 2024, Savanna was ready to deliver the second tranche of 250,000 VCUs, all duly certified on the Verra Registry. Savanna notified DigiCarbon and requested activation of the CarbonChain platform to receive the second tranche submission. DigiCarbon refused to activate the platform, citing the unresolved Tranche 1 dispute and asserting that Savanna was in fundamental breach of the SCPA.

  13. On 10 May 2024, the Nairobi Business Observer, an online financial publication, published an article titled "Questions Raised Over Mau Forest Carbon Project Accounting" (the "Article"), citing allegations by an unnamed civil society source that the Mau Project's carbon accounting figures had been inflated by approximately 15% due to alleged baseline manipulation. No regulatory authority had opened any formal investigation. Verra confirmed in a public statement on 12 May 2024 that it had received no formal complaint regarding the Mau Project and that the project's certification remained valid and in good standing. (Claimant Exhibit C 7)

  14. On 15 May 2024, DigiCarbon sent Savanna a formal notice declaring the SCPA terminated on three stated grounds: (i) Savanna's delivery of non-compliant VCUs in Tranche 1, constituting a fundamental breach; (ii) integrity concerns regarding the Mau Project raised by the Article; and (iii) alleged impracticability of performance arising from Kenya's Carbon Markets Regulations, 2024. DigiCarbon gave no prior written force majeure notice under Clause 12 of the SCPA.

  15. Following DigiCarbon's termination, Savanna was left with 180,000 rejected Tranche 1 VCUs and 250,000 undelivered Tranche 2 VCUs — 430,000 VCUs in total — for which no payment had been received. The ongoing carrying cost of maintaining these VCUs in the Verra Registry, including project maintenance, monitoring, and verification expenses, amounted to KES 500 (approximately USD 3.85) per VCU per month, creating strong commercial pressure to sell promptly. Moreover, Savanna required the greenhouse capacity... — the verification capacity and registry space for a new cycle of VCU issuances scheduled to begin in May 2024.

  16. On 1 June 2024, after approaching six potential buyers, Savanna entered into a cover sale with ClimateFirst Partners Ltd ("ClimateFirst"), an independent Kenyan impact investment fund, for all 430,000 remaining VCUs at 8 GRT per VCU — the best price obtainable in a market experiencing downward pressure due to high-profile global controversies over carbon credit quality. ClimateFirst had no prior corporate relationship with Savanna.

  17. On 15 June 2024, Savanna initiated mediation proceedings before the Nairobi Centre for International Mediation. The mediation terminated on 31 July 2024 without resolution, after the parties confirmed an impasse.

  18. On 1 September 2024, Savanna filed this Notice of Arbitration.

LEGAL EVALUATION

Procedure

  1. The Arbitral Tribunal has jurisdiction pursuant to Clause 15 of the SCPA, which provides:

"Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, including any question regarding its existence, validity or termination, shall be referred to and finally resolved by arbitration administered by the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration ("NCIA") in accordance with the current Arbitration Rules of the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration ("NCIA Arbitration Rules"). The seat of the arbitration shall be Nairobi, Kenya. The Tribunal shall consist of three arbitrators. The language of the arbitration shall be English. This Agreement is governed by the laws of Kenya."

  1. The parties agreed to resolve their disputes under the NCIA Arbitration Rules current at the time of contracting, that is, the NCIA Arbitration Rules 2015. The NCIA Rules 2024 do not govern this arbitration. DigiCarbon contends otherwise; Savanna contests that position.

  2. To fund this arbitration, Savanna has obtained litigation financing from AfriArb Capital Ltd (Nairobi) and adverse cost insurance from InsureTrade Ltd (Nairobi), both of which are disclosed in this Notice.

Substance

  1. DigiCarbon breached the SCPA by: (a) wrongfully refusing to release payment for 180,000 fully VCS-compliant Tranche 1 VCUs, relying on an opaque and biased CarbonAI determination that contradicted Verra's certification and Bureau Veritas's independent re-verification; and (b) refusing to accept the second tranche of 250,000 VCUs and wrongfully terminating the SCPA.

  2. CarbonAI's determination is not contractually final or conclusive in the face of Verra certification. Clause 8.2 of the SCPA must be read consistently with Clause 5.2 (which designates VCS Methodology VM0015 as the quality standard) and Clause 8.3 (which preserves Verra as the referee in cases of conflict). Where CarbonAI's parameters are not grounded in any published VCS Methodology — as confirmed by Bureau Veritas — the contractually agreed standard (VCS) prevails.

  3. None of DigiCarbon's stated grounds for termination are valid: (i) Savanna did not breach the SCPA; (ii) the Article is unverified and Verra confirmed no complaint; and (iii) the CMR 2024 does not render performance impracticable — the Mau Project was registered under the CMR 2024 on 1 February 2024, prior to the Tranche 2 delivery date.

  4. Following DigiCarbon's breach and wrongful termination, Savanna effected a reasonable cover sale on 1 June 2024 at 8 GRT per VCU. Savanna is entitled to damages equal to the difference between the contract price (12 GRT per VCU) and the cover sale price (8 GRT per VCU) multiplied by 430,000 VCUs: 1,720,000 GRT. At the exchange rate prevailing on the date of DigiCarbon's termination notice (15 May 2024), being USD 2.50 per GRT, this equates to USD 4,300,000. Savanna claims this sum in USD given GRT's volatility.

  5. Savanna reserves the right to claim further damages, including reasonable financing costs, as appropriate.

CLAIMANT EXHIBIT C 1

Witness Statement of Dr. Amara Njoroge

  1. I am the Chief Executive Officer of Savanna Carbon Solutions Ltd ("Savanna"), a position I have held since the company's founding in 2018. I hold a PhD in Environmental Science from the University of Nairobi and a Master's degree in Carbon Finance from the University of Edinburgh. I began my career in forest conservation at the Kenya Forest Service and subsequently worked in voluntary carbon market development for ten years before co-founding Savanna.

  2. Savanna's Mau Forest Carbon Conservation Project ("Mau Project") has been certified by Verra under the Verified Carbon Standard since 2019. The project has been independently verified three times by different Verra-accredited VVBs. Each verification has confirmed the project's compliance with VCS Methodology VM0015 (Avoided Unplanned Deforestation, version 1.3). The most recent verification, completed by EcoAudit Africa Ltd in July 2023, confirmed the issuance of 612,447 VCUs for the 2022 verification period.

  3. In January 2023, I received a communication from Mr. Lars Voss, CEO of DigiCarbon International PLC, expressing DigiCarbon's interest in purchasing large volumes of Mau Project VCUs for resale on its CarbonChain platform. Mr. Voss described DigiCarbon as a leading digital carbon market operator that tokenizes voluntary carbon credits as GreenTokens (GRT) for institutional buyers seeking blockchain-based carbon offsetting solutions.

  4. During our negotiations in February 2023, I asked Mr. Voss and Ms. Yuki Tanaka, DigiCarbon's Chief Technology Officer, to explain how CarbonAI would evaluate our VCUs. Ms. Tanaka stated clearly that CarbonAI "directly implements Verra's VCS methodology" and that any VCU bearing a valid Verra Registry certificate and independent verification report "would, as a matter of course, pass CarbonAI verification." I relied on this representation. Ms. Tanaka's email of 20 February 2023 confirmed that a valid Verra Registry certificate and third-party verification report are "the primary inputs to CarbonAI's assessment." I understood "primary inputs" to mean the determinative inputs — the VCS certification was the standard against which CarbonAI measured compliance.

  5. DigiCarbon proposed Clause 8.2 of the SCPA, under which CarbonAI's determination would be "conclusive" for payment purposes. I accepted this clause only because DigiCarbon had represented that CarbonAI faithfully implemented the VCS methodology and because Clause 8.3 was included as a safeguard: where there was a material conflict between CarbonAI's determination and a valid Verra certificate, the parties agreed to refer the matter to Verra within 30 days.

  6. Following the Rejection Report of 5 October 2023, I reviewed the three parameters CarbonAI had flagged. The "Non-Additionality Risk Index," "Permanence Concern Index," and "Anomalous Baseline Deviation" are not defined metrics under VCS Methodology VM0015 or any other published Verra VCS methodology. I consulted with Verra's technical team directly, and they confirmed that these are not VCS standard reporting parameters. There was therefore a material conflict between CarbonAI's determination and the VCS certification, triggering Clause 8.3.

  7. I engaged Bureau Veritas Kenya Ltd to re-verify the 250,000 Tranche 1 VCUs. Their report of 15 November 2023 confirmed full VCS compliance. I submitted this report to DigiCarbon and, separately, invoked Clause 8.3 by requesting Verra to review the conflict. Verra declined to intervene in a buyer-seller AI dispute. Despite this, I considered that Savanna had done all it reasonably could under Clause 8.3.

  8. Regarding the Article published on 10 May 2024 alleging inflated carbon accounting: the allegations were anonymous and sourced to a single unnamed NGO. Verra confirmed on 12 May 2024 that no complaint had been received and that our certification remains valid. All three independent verifications of the Mau Project have been conducted by different Verra-accredited bodies and each has confirmed the integrity of our carbon accounting methodology.

  9. The cover sale with ClimateFirst Partners Ltd on 1 June 2024 at 8 GRT per VCU was the best price available in the market at that time for a bulk transaction of 430,000 VCUs. I approached six potential buyers. The voluntary carbon market was under significant downward pressure globally due to unrelated controversies. ClimateFirst's offer of 8 GRT per VCU was the highest received.

1. What are the exact provisions of the NCIA Rules relevant to these proceedings?

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2015, Rule 1.2: "These Rules shall apply to all arbitrations commenced under the auspices of the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration where the parties have agreed to arbitration under these Rules or under the Rules of the NCIA."

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2015, Rule 26 (Case Management Powers): "The Tribunal may make such orders and give such directions as it considers appropriate to facilitate the fair, efficient and expeditious resolution of the dispute, including orders for the production and inspection of documents and other evidence."

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2024, Rule 1.4: "These Rules shall apply to any arbitration administered by the NCIA that is commenced on or after 1 January 2024, unless the parties have otherwise agreed in writing."

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2024, Rule 38 (AI Systems in Arbitral Proceedings):

"38.1 Where an AI system has been used by a party in the performance of the contract in dispute, the Tribunal may, upon application by the other party, order the producing party to disclose the AI system's decision logic, evaluation parameters, and bias assessment methodology, to the extent the Tribunal finds necessary for a fair determination of the dispute.
38.2 Such disclosure shall be subject to appropriate confidentiality protections as the Tribunal may order in its discretion, including production to the Tribunal only or to the other party subject to a confidentiality undertaking.

38.4 A party that has used an AI system in contractual performance shall, upon request, disclose to the other party whether the AI system's parameters or thresholds may have been modified between the contract date and the date of performance."

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2024, Rule 39 (Smart Contracts as Evidence):

"39.1 A smart contract execution log is admissible as evidence of the automated functions it performed.
39.2 A smart contract execution log does not, of itself, constitute conclusive evidence of performance of an underlying contractual obligation where such obligation is defined by the express terms of the parties' written agreement.
39.3 The Tribunal shall weigh smart contract evidence in light of all terms of the parties' agreement and applicable law."

NCIA Arbitration Rules 2024, Rule 40 (Digital Asset Valuation):

"40.1 Where a party claims damages denominated in or by reference to a virtual asset, the Tribunal shall determine the applicable valuation methodology having regard to: (a) the nature and characteristics of the virtual asset; (b) prevailing market rates at the relevant valuation date; (c) the law of the seat on legal tender and currency; and (d) the applicable principle of full compensation.
